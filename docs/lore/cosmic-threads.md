# Cosmic Threads — «Космические нити»

Dimension lore. Slug `cosmic-threads`. Sampled palette, most-used first:
`#424344` wash grey · `#2E2C28` warm ink · `#A5A5A5` mid grey · `#D5D6D9`
gouache white · `#1A1815` void black · `#737271` stone grey. Terrain RIDGE,
fog near 25 far 220 — close, on purpose. Everything below is buildable from
these six greys only. The painting hangs last in the study; the world is
allowed to know why, quietly.

## 1. The painting, read closely

Ink and gouache, no colour anywhere. On a dark wash field, a dozen and more
spheres, each patterned differently: one carries long straight chords like
jackstraws; one is banded and wears a Saturn ring; one holds a spoked
wheel; one a patch of checkerboard, one of brick; one is stippled, one
striped, one grained with concentric rings like a fingerprint; one is
scribbled with spirals, and one, low left, wears rows of small watching
eyes. Down the middle runs a pillar: a white looping vessel at the base, a
funnel, a stack of rippled tiers alternating dots and hatching, and at the
top a big net-latticed sphere holding a drift of stippled sand, a small
pale figure perched above it all. Thick white tendrils loop from sphere to
sphere and around the pillar, and along the bottom a string of small beads
— marbled, faceted, striped — rides one thread like a necklace. No sphere
repeats a pattern. No sphere is unconnected.

## 2. The world's story

You are inside the necklace. The ground is ridge and spire, wash-grey
falling to void-black in the hollows, and the fog sits close — but white
threads run through it, raised roads curving off into the grey, and every
thread has a patterned moon at both ends. That is the one law everybody
here trusts: a thread never dead-ends. Walk one long enough and a shape
thickens out of the fog, its pattern resolves — bands, or bricks, or a
wheel of spokes — and you have arrived somewhere that is about something,
even if it never says what. Every thread, followed far enough, passes the
Spindle at the centre, where the sand sits in its net and the small
watcher keeps its seat. People who have walked other worlds sometimes stop
in front of a moon, sure they have seen that pattern before, somewhere,
in colour. The world lets them frown.

## 3. Signature structures

**Pattern-moons — Узорные луны.** Voxel spheres, radius 6–14, grounded on
ridge crests: a shell of white `#D5D6D9` or mid grey `#A5A5A5` carrying one
seeded pattern in ink `#2E2C28` and void `#1A1815` blocks — bands, chords,
grid, brick, stipple, spiral, spokes, rings, eyes. One pattern per moon.
Lore: a world seen from outside; the pattern is what it is about.

**Threads — Нити.** The roads: raised runs of white `#D5D6D9` blocks, two
wide, one to three blocks proud of the terrain, following seeded S-curve
splines moon to moon, crossing hollows on stone-grey `#737271` stilts.
Lore: the connective tissue. A thread reaching a moon wraps a half loop
around it before leaving — the painting's tendrils do, so ours do.

**The Spindle — Веретено.** One per world, at origin, ~40 blocks tall: a
looping white vessel with two block-arch handles, a narrow funnel waist,
four rippled tiers alternating stipple and hatch, and at the top a lattice
sphere of white struts over void, its lower third filled with mid-grey
`#A5A5A5` sand blocks. On the very top, a small pale figure, three blocks,
seated. Lore: where all threads eventually lead. Nobody knows what the
sand times. The figure watches.

**Bead-strings — Бусы.** Low threads near the valley floors strung with
small spheres, radius 1–2, a dozen to a string, each wearing a tiny
two-colour pattern. Lore: little worlds, or worlds not grown yet. You can
step over them; most people step along.

## 4. Ambient life

**Thread-pulses — Бегунки.** Pale instanced motes travelling the threads —
a single `#D5D6D9` point sliding along the spline at walking pace, passing
without stopping. Messages, presumably. Densest near the Spindle.

**Ink-flecks — Мошки-кляксы.** Small dark boid dots, five to nine, circling
each moon low and slow like flecks shaken off a pen. Each flock is its
moon's; they never cross the void.

## 5. Activities

1. **Walk a thread out.** Pick a thread and follow it to the far end. The
   fog is close, so the destination earns itself: silhouette first, then
   pattern, then arrival. The law holds — there is always a moon.
2. **The collection.** Walking every moon is the world's long game. Some
   patterns feel oddly familiar — bands like a striped sea, a spoked
   wheel, joined dark circles like a molecule, rows of small sleepy eyes —
   all in grey. The world never says why.
3. **Look back at your own moon.** From the moon where you arrive, the
   moon you left finally shows its whole pattern across the fog. You can
   never quite read the one you are standing on. That is not a bug.
4. **Climb the Spindle.** Handles, waist, tiers, lattice. From the seat
   beside the watcher, the threads lay themselves out below like a drawing
   of everything. Sit a while.
5. **String the beads.** Walk a bead-string end to end without stepping
   off the thread. Smallest road in the world, hardest to walk.

## 7. What would ruin it

- **Outer space.** No starfield, nebula glow, purple, or planets in the
  sky. The painting's void is ink wash on paper, close and matte; the fog
  at 25 blocks *is* the void. Looks like a space level, stops being his.
- **Explaining the wink.** No signs naming the other dimensions, no portal
  moons, no "you are here" map. A pattern that reminds you is the whole
  device; a label would turn the last painting's quiet joke into a lobby.
- **Mystic threads.** No pulsing energy, hum, or life-force shader. The
  threads are gouache — string, rope, road. A thread-pulse is a mote, not
  a spirit. The network is alive the way a postal route is alive.
