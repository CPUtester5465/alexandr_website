# Headwind — Встречный ветер

Lore for the dimension `headwind`. Source painting: `art-originals/Headwind.png`.
Palette (sampled, most-used first): `#9EA2A5` 33% sky-grey · `#768A97` 27% mid
sea · `#314454` 18% deep slate · `#546571` 13% slate · `#DAC8BF` 8% sail-cream ·
`#5E4238` 1% hull-brown. The last two are the only warmth in the world and are
rationed like the study's red.

## 1. The painting, read closely

One cutter, bow into it. The mast is a single thin brown stroke; off it hang
three panels of canvas — two headsails stacked forward, the tall main aft — and
every panel is bellied, not slack. The white of the sails is not white: it is a
warm cream against a sky that has no warmth in it at all. The hull is a low dark
crescent, one knife-line of foam breaking along it. What carries the picture is
the knife work: the paint is thickest in the upper-left sky and the lower-left
sea — one diagonal of pressure crossing the canvas — while the right side is
scraped nearly smooth. The wind is not depicted. It is *built*, in ridges of
paint you could catch a fingernail on. Nothing else is in the frame. No land,
no birds, no second boat. A vessel, the sea, and the weather leaning on both.

## 2. The world's story

This is the sea the cutter is crossing, walked instead of sailed. The wind here
never started and never stops: it comes out of the fog line to windward, always
from the same quarter, and it leans on everything — on the long slate swells, on
the walker, on the few things people have managed to raise. It is the antagonist
because it concedes nothing; it is the teacher because it can be read, and read
well it can be *used* — angled against, sheltered from, finally turned and
ridden. Whoever crossed before you learned this, and left the evidence standing.
The world stays near-monochrome on purpose: four slates and a grey, so that the
one cream sail-cloth and the one brown timber read like a shout. Colour here is
not scenery. It is proof that someone made an effort against the wind and won.

## 3. Signature structures

All seeded along the wind axis; every one of them agrees about the direction.

**Heeled masts — Кренящиеся мачты.** Bare mast-poles, 8–14 blocks tall, one
block thick in hull-brown `#5E4238`, every one leaning the same few degrees
downwind — instanced stem columns with a per-course lateral offset, no bend
maths needed. At the head, 2–3 blocks of sail-cream `#DAC8BF` canvas, strung
taut on the lee side. Sparse (spacing ~16, density ~0.4). Lore: the trail of
crossings before yours; a compass you cannot misread, because the wind set it.

**Lee walls — Заветрия.** Low dry-stone arcs of deep slate `#314454`, one block
thick, 2–3 high, 5–9 long, curved with the concave face downwind — a stamped
arc of blocks on the swell-tops, capped with a single course of pale `#9EA2A5`.
Inside the arc the wind mechanic drops to near zero: standing in a lee is the
one place in the world where walking feels normal. Lore: shelters raised by
tired hands, left unlocked for whoever comes next.

**Vehas — Вехи.** A веха is a real seamark: a spar with a topmark. Here, a
2-block hull-brown post with one sail-cream block at the tip, set in seeded
lines running dead upwind, one every 20–30 blocks — two instanced boxes. Lore:
distance won against the wind, staked; a veha line always ends at a lee wall.

**Crest caps — Барашки.** The painting's impasto foam: where ridged swells
crest windward, the top course of terrain swaps to pale `#9EA2A5` — a
generation-time material rule, not a structure mesh. Sailors call whitecaps
барашки, "little lambs". Lore: the sea showing its teeth exactly where the
wind bites hardest, so the ground itself tells you where the gusts live.

## 4. Ambient life

Almost nothing lives in permanent wind, and what does has stopped fighting it.
**Hover-gulls (висящие чайки):** pale `#9EA2A5` block-birds that face the wind
and beat just hard enough to stay still — the only creatures in the world, and
they hang like nails driven into the air; against the grey sky you find them by
their flicker, not their colour. **Spindrift (водяная пыль):** seeded particle
streaks of pale grey skimming downwind off the crest caps, low and fast — the
painting's knife ridges, moving. **Loose canvas (обрывки паруса):** rare cream
scraps snagged on a mast, snapping in the gust cycle. Nothing here speaks.

## 5. Activities

The wind is a seeded field (steady push + gust cycles), so every one of these
works anywhere in the infinite world, forever, without authored content.

1. **Tacking — Лавировка.** Walking dead upwind is the slowest line in the
   world. Walking 40° off it is markedly faster — so the fastest way windward is
   a zigzag, tack by tack, exactly as a sailboat beats. Nobody explains this.
   The wind mechanic *is* the tutorial, and figuring it out is the world's one
   secret. (Wind as substrate.)
2. **Lee-hopping — По заветриям.** Cross long distances by chaining shelters:
   sprint the exposed stretch, rest where the wind can't reach, read the crest
   caps to pick the next gap between gusts. Route-finding as breathing rhythm.
   (Wind as substrate.)
3. **Running downwind — Фордевинд.** Turn your back on the whole problem and
   the wind becomes an engine: the fastest travel in the dimension, effortless
   and slightly out of control — and always *away* from where the vehas point.
   The world's one bargain: speed, or progress. (Wind as substrate.)
4. **Walking the veha line — По вехам.** Follow a seeded veha line to windward,
   each post a block of distance won, until it delivers you to its lee wall.
5. **Counting lambs — Считать барашков.** From inside a lee, watch the crest
   caps whiten and fade until you know the gust period, then go back out.

## 7. Three kitsch traps

1. **Storm theatre.** No lightning, no rain, no darkening sky, no thunder. The
   painting is a steady grey working day of pressure, not a tempest. The moment
   the weather performs, the wind stops being felt and starts being watched.
2. **Colour rescue.** No sunset break, no red pennant, no golden hour to
   "relieve" the monochrome. The near-monochrome is the subject. Cream and
   hull-brown are the entire warm budget, and they are already spent.
3. **Wreck romance.** No sunken ships, no skeletons, no ominous ruins — grimdark
   is kitsch in a minor key. The sea in the painting holds one vessel, upright
   and under sail. Everything left standing here was won, not lost.
