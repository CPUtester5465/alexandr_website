# Sailing the Divide — «Под парусом по разлому»

Dimension lore. Slug `divide`. Sampled palette, most-used first: `#454346`
storm charcoal · `#6690BE` calm blue · `#D2623E` molten orange · `#E99546`
amber · `#804B34` hull brown · `#C2B7AB` pale sun-white. Terrain SWELL, sky
`#6690BE`. Everything below is buildable from these six colours only.

## 1. The painting, read closely

The sky is cut in two along a diagonal: upper left, clear blue with thick
white impasto clouds; upper right, a charcoal-black mass shot through with
streaks of pink and deep blue. Right on the cut, a band of orange and amber
burns across the whole canvas, and a pale, almost white sun sits low in it,
left of centre. Two dark-hulled sailboats — one small and far at the left,
one nearer at centre-right — sail the molten reflection the sun lays on the
water. The sea quietly reverses the sky: under the calm half the water is at
its darkest green-black, while under the storm the water is the part flecked
with bright blue. Neither boat flees the storm; both keep to the lit seam.

## 2. The world's story

This is a sea-world with a fixed weather line running through it — calm sky
on one side, storm sky on the other, forever, the way the painting holds
both at once. The obvious thing about such a line is that it divides. The
non-obvious thing, and the thing everyone here lives by, is that **all the
light lives on the line**: the sun only burns where the two skies touch, so
the seam is not a border to be avoided but the only road. Worse — or better —
the water plays a trick: the calm side's sea is the dark one, and the storm
side's sea is the one that glitters. So nobody gets to choose comfort. If
you want quiet air you sail over black water; if you want bright water you
sail under a black sky; the only place with both light and calm is the seam
itself, and it is narrow. The seam-dwellers are not brave and not reckless —
just people who noticed where the sun actually is, and moved onto it.

## 3. Signature structures

**Line-masts — Створные столбы.** Tall thin pillars (1×1 footprint, 6–18
blocks) in hull brown with one amber `#E99546` cap block and one pale
`#C2B7AB` band at head height, standing in slow procession along the seam,
each raised where the last fades into fog. Lore: the seam-dwellers'
navigation — you never search for the road, you walk mast to mast. Three to
nine players tall: furniture of the route, not a monument.

**Half-houses — Дома-на-шве.** Small cabins (roughly 5×4×4) built exactly
astride the line, roof ridge running along it: the calm-facing half in blue
`#6690BE` over charcoal, the storm-facing half in charcoal over blue — each
half wearing the *other* side's water colour, the way the sea does — with one
molten `#D2623E` doorway block. Lore: a seam-dweller sleeps with one ear in
each weather, and their house admits it. Eaves just above head height; climb
the ridge and stand with a foot in each sky.

**Ember piers — Янтарные причалы.** Short two-wide jetties of hull-brown
blocks running from a swell-crest toward the sun-road, the last two planks
amber as if the reflection had caught them. Lore: where boats put in — nobody
moors *at* the light; you moor as near as the wood can stand.

**Rib-wrecks — Рёбра кораблей.** Storm side only, far from the seam: arcs of
hull-brown blocks half-buried in the swells, three to five ribs, no deck, no
mast — the shape of a boat with everything soft gone. Lore: boats that left
the road and kept going. Not graves, not haunted — the world's quiet
arithmetic about how far the light reaches.

## 4. Ambient life

**Sail-gulls — Чайки-паруса.** Flat pale-`#C2B7AB`-triangle boids (one quad
each, instanced), flocking low along the seam in groups of seven to twelve,
banking amber-side-up in the sunset band. They ride the swells' shape rather
than flapping — small sails that got free.

**Road-motes — Искры дорожки.** Slow instanced amber points rising a block
or two off the molten reflection and going out, like the water letting go of
heat. Dense only on the sun-road; absent everywhere else, so the road glows
in the corner of your eye before you can see it.

## 5. Activities

1. **Walk the road.** Follow the seam mast to mast; each line-mast's amber
   cap lights (accent → accentLit) as you pass. The lit masts behind you are
   the record of your voyage in that world-integer.
2. **The storm dare.** Leave the seam into the storm side as far as your
   nerve holds — colour thins toward charcoal, the wind leans on you. Touch
   a rib-wreck; come back. The lit masts show the way home.
3. **Trade sides.** Cross from calm sky over dark water to dark sky over lit
   water and feel the world swap: same swells, opposite light. No reward
   except understanding the painting, which is the reward.
4. **Race the gulls.** Sprint the sun-road from pier to pier against a
   sail-gull flock. They hold the seam exactly; you may cut a swell and
   cheat. They do not mind.
5. **Find the sun-pool.** Somewhere on the seam the reflection widens into a
   pool of pale `#C2B7AB` blocks — the one place in the world where the
   ground itself is the sun's colour. One per region, seeded. Stand in it.

## 7. What would ruin it

- **Making the storm evil.** The painted storm carries pink and bright blue;
  it is weather, not a villain. No lightning demons, no damage on crossing.
  The moment one side is "the bad side", the painting's idea — two boats
  choosing the line between — collapses into a fantasy map.
- **VFX at the seam.** No glowing wall, particle curtain, or portal shimmer.
  The painting marks the divide with sunset light it already has; the world
  marks it with masts and the sun-road. Any colour outside the six sampled
  hexes is a lie about the painting.
- **Epic register.** No prophecy, no chosen sailor, no skulls on the wrecks,
  no quest text. Two small boats, small houses, a dare children do once. The
  courage in the painting is eleven years old, and that is its dignity.
