# Desert Table, Curved Voices — Пустынный стол, изогнутые голоса

Dimension lore for slug `desert-table`. Source: `art-originals/Desert-Table-Curved-Voices.png`.
Palette (sampled, most-used first): `#90414B` madder curtain · `#737A5B` cactus/floor green ·
`#B1A266` woven ochre · `#7A568B` pitcher violet · `#473E48` ink outline · `#96A1B2` glass blue.
Spec facts to honour: MEADOW terrain, SPARSE_FLOWERS, fog 55/300, and sky = `#90414B` —
the sky of this world is the curtain's own red. Keep that; it is the painting's ceiling.

## 1. The painting, read closely

Two curtains sag from the top edge in deep madder pleats, every fold hatched in herringbone —
even the softest thing in the room is drawn as a grid. Behind them a wall of woven ochre,
basketweave strokes going four ways; below, a floor of green diamond tiles. Against all that
geometry stand the curves: a tall lidded pitcher in lavender and magenta ribbons that flow like
poured cream, leaning right; a smaller open-mouthed pitcher in glass-blue, hip cocked, leaning
left, one orange flower craning out of its mouth on a bent stem. They face each other. They are
talking. A cactus in a violet-striped ribbed pot raises stepped, right-angled arms — the one
figure in the room built on the grid's terms — and still wears a soft blue flower at the top.
Down front: a stemmed glass of berry-dark drink on a wavy blue stem, and three citrus wedges
set out on the tiles. Nobody is in the picture, but everything is arranged *for* somebody.
That is the subject: hospitality, performed by the furniture.

## 2. The world's story

You arrive table-sized on a table that never ends — green tiled ground rolling away in low
meadow swells, the madder curtain hanging where a sky should be, fog the colour of the far
folds. The table is set, and has always been set, for a guest who is always just about to
arrive; the world's whole occupation is being ready. Its residents are the pitchers, and they
speak in curves: not words but bent lines of colour that rise from their open mouths, drift,
and settle — a language of gesture, the same one their bodies are made of. The grids (tiles,
weave, the cactus's right angles) are the room holding still; the curves are the room being
glad to see you. The player is the guest. Everything leans slightly toward them.

## 3. Signature structures

**The Speakers — Кувшины-собеседники.** Pitcher-towers, 8–14 blocks tall, always generated in
leaning pairs a few blocks apart, each pair angled toward its partner (posture, never faces).
Build: stacked block ellipses — flared foot, swollen belly, pinched waist, open lip — banded in
vertical ribbons of `#7A568B` violet and `#96A1B2` blue with `#473E48` seam columns; the taller
of a pair carries a dome lid with a single sphere-knob. Hollow: a 2×2 shaft lets the player
climb down inside. Lore: the hosts. A pair mid-conversation, paused because you walked in.

**Waymark Cacti — Кактусы-указатели.** Build: a terraced ribbed pot (stacked rings alternating
`#90414B` and `#7A568B`, each ring one block wider than the last going down), ochre `#B1A266`
soil cap, then a `#737A5B` green trunk with 2–4 arms that turn only in right angles — pure
voxel joy, straight from the painting — crowned with one `#96A1B2` bloom block. 6–12 blocks.
Lore: the one resident who learned the grid's language; its arms point (seeded) toward the
nearest Speaker pair. The world's signposts.

**Citrus Wedges — Ломтики цитруса.** Build: quarter-cylinder lying flat, 3–5 blocks of radius:
an outer rind arc of `#B1A266` ochre, inner radial segments of paler blocks with `#473E48`
partition lines. Low, climbable, benches at guest scale. Instanced; scattered near Speakers
like something already served. Lore: the offering — proof the table was set for you.

**Curtain Banners — Занавесы.** Build: paired `#473E48` poles with a catenary sag of madder
`#90414B` blocks strung between them, 2 blocks thick, the sag quantised into steps; sparse,
on ridgelines, framing long views the way the painted curtains frame the still life. Lore:
the edges of the theatre. Beyond every banner, more table — the show has no backstage.

## 4. Ambient life

The **curve-voices** themselves: single blocks of `#7A568B` and `#96A1B2` that rise from
Speaker mouths and drift along seeded arcs before fading — the pitchers' conversation, visible,
denser when the player stands between a pair. **Berry motes** — occasional `#90414B` specks
that hop once and settle, spilled from some glass. And the spec's own sparse flowers read here
as the orange bloom escaping the blue pitcher: stray table-flowers that grew where a voice
landed. No creatures with eyes. The room is alive; nothing in it is an animal.

## 5. Activities

**Answer in curves.** Stand between a Speaker pair; their voice-arcs form a seeded phrase.
Walking a path that mirrors the arc (the trail glows briefly) makes both pitchers dip toward
you — the world's version of being understood. Every pair's phrase derives from its position.

**Serve the table.** Citrus wedges can be nudged, one block at a time. Rolling one into the
open circle between a Speaker pair completes a "setting"; the pair's voices arc higher for a
while. Infinite: every pair, forever, will accept a wedge.

**Follow the arms.** Waymark Cacti point toward Speakers; Speakers' lean hints at the next
cactus. Chaining them is a walk that always has a next step — a seeded thread of hospitality
across the whole tabletop, never a finish line.

**Go down inside.** Climb a lidded Speaker and descend the shaft: a small banded room, the
voice-blocks drifting up past you from below. Nothing to collect. It is just good in there.

**Walk to the curtain.** Pick a Curtain Banner on the horizon and reach it. There is always
another beyond, redder in the fog. The point is the leaning company you pass on the way.

## 7. Three kitsch traps

**No faces.** The painting anthropomorphises entirely through posture — a lean, a cocked hip,
an open mouth that is genuinely a spout. The moment a pitcher gets eyes or a smile it becomes
a teapot mascot. Personality lives in the tilt angle, nowhere else.

**No desert.** Despite the title: no sand, dunes, skulls, tumbleweed, sombrero, cow-horn — the
"desert" is one potted cactus in a warm room. The world is domestic theatre; import even one
Western prop and it collapses into a themed restaurant.

**No words.** The curve-voices must never resolve into letters, speech bubbles, or musical
notes. The painting's whole idea is a language made of shape; captioning it is defacing it.
If a voice needs to "mean" something, it means it by where it bends.
