# Invention — Изобретательство

Subject-dimension lore. Slug `invention`. No painting: the palette is the study's own
materials — `#EDE6D2` paper · `#C8B392` plaster · `#8A8578` stone · `#4A4741` graphite ·
`#23241F` ink — plus rationed `#AC7036` brass, which may touch nothing but a working
mechanism. Spec facts: STILL terrain (table-flat), MACHINES structures (stepped,
hollow, walk-through), fog 45/300, sky = `#EDE6D2` — the sky is the paper.

## 1. The record, and the man in the contest's name

Alexandr won «День Кулибина» — Kulibin Day, the young inventors' and technicians'
competition at Southwest State University, Kursk, 2026, judged by university faculty
against entrants from across the oblast. The day is named for Ivan Kulibin
(1735–1818), a self-taught mechanic from Nizhny Novgorod, so famous that «кулибин»,
lower-case, is still what Russians call anyone who builds clever things by hand. He made
a clock the size and shape of a goose egg with a tiny automaton theatre inside and
presented it to Catherine II in 1769; it still runs, in the Hermitage. He designed a
single wooden arch to cross the Neva in one 298-metre span; his one-tenth-scale model
passed the Academy of Sciences' load test in 1776, but the bridge was never built. He
also built the vodokhod, a ship that used the river's own current to haul itself
upstream (Neva, 1782), a mirror-facet lantern turning one candle into a beam visible
for miles (1779), and an optical telegraph (1794) — most of it filed away unused.
Winning the day named after him means: same country, same kind of mind.

## 2. The world's story

This is the workshop-country where Kulibin's things finally got built. The ground is
one endless workbench — flat plaster, true and level, because a bench that isn't level
can't be trusted with a measurement — under a sky of clean drawing paper, and standing
on it, spaced like projects in progress, are the machines: stepped, hollow, honest
about their construction, every one traceable to something Kulibin actually made or
proposed. Nothing here was invented by us; the world's only liberty is the word *yes*.
Nothing stands behind glass — a machine you may not touch is a machine you cannot
learn — and brass sits exactly where a mechanism does its work, so you always find the
moving part by colour.

## 3. Signature structures

**The Arch — Однопролётный мост.** The single-arch bridge, built at last, at many
scales — from a model you step over to a span you climb. Build: a rising-falling arc of
graphite blocks doubled into upper and lower chords with short lattice blocks between
(his design was a timber truss; voxel steps show it honestly), landing on heavy stone
abutment piles; brass only at the bearing blocks where arch meets abutment. Arches
cross other machines or nothing at all: an arch does not need a river to be true.
History: one 298 m leap over the Neva; the model held the 1776 test load; never built
— until here. Lore: the landmark. Every arch is *yes*.

**The Egg-Clocks — Часы яичной фигуры.** Build: a stack of block-ellipse rings — paper
shell, plaster bands — 6–10 blocks tall on a graphite stand, with one brass ring that
visibly rotates, one block-step at a time: the world's steadiest motion. On the seeded
hour two shell blocks swing open; inside, a hollow ink-dark room with a single brass
block turning — the theatre. History: three years of work, presented in 1769; it staged
a tiny automaton play on the hour, and still runs in the Hermitage. Lore: the
timekeepers — the world ticks in phase with its eggs.

**The Waterwalkers — Водоходы.** Build: a blunt hull of stone and graphite flat on the
bench, two tall graphite side wheels — brass only at the axles — turning slowly; from
the bow a cable-line of single ink blocks runs low to a brass-capped anchor post twenty
blocks ahead. Wheels turn, hull creeps along its cable, arrives, and the cable re-lays
to the next seeded post: a ship walking its own rope forever. History: the current
turned the wheels, the wheels wound the anchor rope — the river pulled the ship
upstream against itself; it worked, and was never adopted. Lore: the country's freight.

**The Lanterns — Кулибинские фонари.** Build: a plain graphite mast, 6–12 blocks, with
a head of paper-pale blocks stepped into a shallow dish — the facet mirror — and one
brass block at its focus: the single candle. The head swings through a seeded arc, and
whichever machine it faces has its brass lit (accent → accentLit) while the lantern
holds on it. No beam geometry, no glow: light appears only where it lands. History:
hundreds of facets behind one weak flame, a beam reported visible for miles. Lore: the
night-watch — machines take turns being seen.

## 4. Ambient life

**Shavings — стружка.** Small plaster-and-paper curl blocks drifting up from any moving
machine, hopping once and fading: the only dust here is work-dust. **The tick — ход.**
Each machine's brass pulses accentLit in its own seeded period, in phase with its
nearest egg-clock — watch the country work from the open bench. No creatures, no
eyes: in a workshop the life *is* the machines.

## 5. Activities

1. **Load-test the arch.** The 1776 test, re-run forever: pile stone weight-blocks on
   a model arch. It holds — it held then. As blocks go on, the brass bearings burn
   brighter: an arch carries weight by pushing *outward* at its ends, which is why
   the abutments are the heaviest part.
2. **Walk a waterwalker.** Board and ride; from the bow, watch the mechanism whole —
   wheels turn, cable winds, post arrives: the river pulls the ship against the river.
3. **Wind the eggs.** Hold at an egg-clock's stand to wind it — the brass ring turns
   with you — and on the seeded hour the shell opens and the theatre turns for you
   alone. Every egg keeps its own hour.
4. **Chase the lantern.** Swing a lantern's head and hold it on a far machine until its
   brass wakes across the fog; then follow the light to what it found.
5. **The long crossing.** Seeded once per world stands the full-scale Arch, fog hiding
   its far abutment. Walk the whole upper chord, all 298 m. Kulibin never got to.

## 7. Three kitsch traps

- **No steampunk.** No goggles, top hats, rivet ornament, or gears glued on as
  decoration. Kulibin was an engineer, not a genre; every brass block must do work you
  can watch — a gear that turns nothing lies in the colour the site rations hardest.
- **No smoke.** His machines ran on water, weight, spring and candlelight; there is not
  one steam engine in his life's work. Chimneys, pistons and smoke would make a generic
  industrial theme park, and be false about the actual man. If a machine needs power
  here, it hangs a weight or winds a rope.
- **No bitterness.** The true story has an ache — the bridge unbuilt, the vodokhod
  scrapped, the telegraph shelved — and the trap is a museum of grievance: plaques,
  ruins, "they never listened". This world just builds the things, matter-of-factly,
  and lets a boy walk across the bridge. A workshop on a good morning, not a monument.
