# Mathematics — «Математика»

Dimension lore. Slug `mathematics`. A subject world: no painting to sample,
so the palette is the study's materials plus one rationed colour — `#EDE6D2`
paper · `#C8B392` plaster · `#8A8578` stone · `#4A4741` graphite · `#23241F`
ink · `#B3402C` instrument red. Terrain CONSTRUCTED (terraced, squared, on a
true grid), structures LATTICE, sky paper. Red appears **only** on things
that are true and have been checked.

## 2. The world's story

The ground here is not a landscape mathematics happens on; the ground **is
the working** — a proof somebody is still in the middle of. Terraces are its
steps, graphite construction lines lie pressed into the paper plateaus like
pencil never erased, arcs stand half-struck, whole courts sit tiled
mid-thought. In school, red ink belongs to the one who checks; this world
holds the pen the same way: nothing glows, nothing rewards, but when a thing
is true — a point equidistant, a sum that lands, a path that closes — the
world touches it red, once, and leaves it. A finished proof would be a dead
world. This one stays mid-proof on purpose, and every visitor gets the pencil.

## 3. Signature structures

**Arc-gates — Ворота-засечки.** Two graphite centre-posts a dozen blocks
apart; from each, a voxelised circular arc (Bresenham circle, instanced
plaster blocks with graphite edges) struck at equal radius so the arcs cross
overhead. The blocks where the arcs truly intersect — computed, not eyeballed
— are instrument red. The math: the compass construction; the crossing is
equidistant from both centres, two crossings give the perpendicular bisector.
Lore: a gate is how this world says "these two places are the same distance
from here", and it never marks an unmeasured crossing.

**Prime colonnades — Простые колоннады.** Groups of graphite columns on the
grid, each an exact seeded count. Composite counts stand folded into
rectangles — 12 as 3×4, 15 as 3×5 — fold lines pressed into the paper floor.
Prime counts cannot fold: 7, 11, 13 stand in single file, the end column
capped red. The math: a prime makes no rectangle with both sides greater than
one. Lore: the world sorts every number it owns by whether it folds, and
honours the ones that refuse.

**Parquet courts — Паркетные дворы.** Flat plazas floored by one repeated
tile — a tetromino, or an arbitrary quadrilateral — in alternating paper,
plaster and stone, no gaps, no overlaps. No red: a tiling is not a
measurement, it is a fact of the floor. The math: паркеты, the olympiad
staple — any quadrilateral tiles the plane, its four angles summing to 360°
at each vertex. Lore: the world's scrap paper; each court an attempt that worked.

**Fibonacci terraces — Лестница Фибоначчи.** A seeded few terrace runs rise
1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 blocks, treads curling as the spiral of squares, each new
square's side the sum of the previous two. One red block at each corner where
the quarter-arc would be struck — the spiral's measured points, nothing
between. Lore: the world's oldest staircase; nobody built it, the counting did.

## 4. Ambient life

**Tally starlings — Счётные скворцы.** Instanced ink-fleck boids in exact
counts. A flock of twelve keeps splitting — three fours, four threes, two
sixes — and re-merging; a flock of eleven tries, fails every split, and stays
whole. Watched long enough, a divisibility lesson nobody announces.

**Compass beetles — Циркульные жуки.** Small ink-block crawlers, each pacing
a perfect circle around its own stake at its own fixed radius, pressing a
faint ring into the paper ground. Where two rings cross, the crossing is
pressed deeper — never red; the beetles measure nothing, they only walk well.

## 5. Activities

1. **Finish the construction.** An arc-gate stands with both arcs struck and
   the crossing unmarked. Stand where the arcs meet; the world checks your
   distance to both centre-posts — |d₁ − d₂| < ε and d = radius — and if it
   holds, the block under your feet lights red and stays lit for that seed.
2. **Sort the colonnades.** A district of unlabelled column groups; walk to
   the end column of every file you believe is prime. A true prime's cap
   lights red; a composite's stays graphite while its fold lines press into
   the floor to show you the rectangle you missed. Counts seeded.
3. **The magic court.** A 3×3 court of stone pedestals, heights 1–9, each
   once. Walk a row, column or diagonal end to end; if its heights sum to 15,
   a red seam lights along it — eight seams lit and the court is magic. One
   court per region is seeded one swap from true; finding it is the game.
4. **The bridge walk.** Lattice bridges over a terraced gully, Königsberg-
   style. A bridge lights red on first crossing; repeat one and all drop to
   ink. Cross every bridge exactly once and the far post caps red. Seeded
   graphs always have zero or two odd-degree posts — always fair problems.

## 7. What would ruin it

- **Floating glowing equations.** The big one. No symbols in the air, no
  holographic formulas, no chalkboard sky. Mathematics here is *in* the
  geometry — arcs, counts, tilings, paths — or pressed into surfaces like
  pencil into paper. The moment a "√2" floats, this is a screensaver.
- **Math as magic.** No runes, no sacred geometry, no temple of the ancients.
  The register is a well-kept exercise book, monumentalized — a working that
  gets checked, not a mystery that gets worshipped. Wonder comes from the
  thing being true, not from reverb.
- **Decorative numbers and unearned red.** Every count must be real — a file
  of 13 columns has 13 columns; every arc-crossing is computed. Red is the
  checkmark; red on anything unverified is a checkmark on nothing, which is
  exactly the fabricated-awards failure this site exists to bury.
